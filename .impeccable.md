## Design Context

### Users
Notif Manager is for Android users who want fewer routine interruptions without risking missed important notifications. The core job is to hold routine notifications by default, release them at predictable delivery times, and let users define Open hours and priority apps that may interrupt them immediately.

### Brand Personality
Calm, trustworthy, and native. The interface should feel like a focused Android system utility: clear about its current state, predictable about what will interrupt the user, and never ornamental at the expense of comprehension.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use Material 3 and Material You in a controlled, semantic way. Wallpaper-derived colors remain enabled by default on Android 12 and newer, while light and dark appearance must each be internally coherent across the window, system bars, onboarding, dialogs, and app screens. Avoid generic purple fallback styling, decorative metric-card grids, and light surfaces leaking into dark mode or dark surfaces leaking into light mode.

### Design Principles
1. Make notification behavior predictable: always show whether routine notifications are waiting, arriving immediately, or due for delivery.
2. Present one scheduling model: routine notifications wait by default; delivery times release them; entering Open hours releases the queue and allows new interruptions; priority apps always pass through.
3. Never show setup as complete unless Notification access and Post notifications are granted. Refresh permission state immediately after returning from Android settings; Exact timing is optional and labeled accordingly.
4. Prefer native Android interactions and restrained controls: swipe active notification rows to archive, provide Undo, and reserve explicit Restore actions for archived content and accessibility alternatives.
5. Use consistent user-facing vocabulary: Inbox, waiting, delivery time, Open hours, priority apps, archive, and restore. Do not alternate between implementation terms such as batch, clear, instant window, and silent hold boundaries.
6. Keep power-user controls progressive: lead with priority apps and schedule outcomes, and place per-channel overrides and advanced configuration behind secondary disclosure.
